1. PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This policy defines infection prevention and control (IPC) standards for two
high-risk areas within Apollo Hospitals Chennai:

  (a) Operation Theatre (OT) Complex — Blocks A and B, Second Floor
  (b) Intensive Care Units — MICU, SICU, CICU, NICU, and PICU, Third Floor

It aligns with national guidelines issued by the National Centre for Disease
Control (NCDC), the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW), and
international standards set by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the
Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). It also supports compliance
with NABH (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals) Standard IC.1 through IC.7
and JCI (Joint Commission International) IPSG.5.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This policy applies to:
  - Surgeons, anaesthesiologists, and OT technicians
  - ICU intensivists, fellows, and residents
  - All nursing staff in OT and ICU settings
  - Housekeeping, biomedical engineering, and biomedical waste staff
  - Medical students, interns, and observers (with supervisor responsibility)
  - Vendors and equipment maintenance personnel entering controlled areas

3. DEFINITIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  HAI (Healthcare-Associated Infection):
      Infection acquired during the course of receiving healthcare treatment,
      not present or incubating at the time of admission.

  SSI (Surgical Site Infection):
      Infection occurring within 30 days of an operation (or 90 days if an
      implant is placed) and involving the skin, subcutaneous tissue, deep
      soft tissue, or organ/space at the surgical site.

  CLABSI (Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection):
      Primary bloodstream infection in a patient with a central venous catheter
      (CVC) in place for >48 hours with no other identified source.

  CAUTI (Catheter-Associated Urinary Tract Infection):
      Urinary tract infection in a patient who had an indwelling urinary catheter
      in place for >2 calendar days at the time of infection.

  VAP (Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia):
      Pneumonia occurring in a patient who has been on mechanical ventilation
      for more than 48-72 hours.

  HICO: Hospital Infection Control Officer

  Restricted Zone: Area requiring scrubs, cap, mask, and shoe covers (OT corridor).
  Sterile Zone: Area requiring full sterile attire (OT interior, scrub sink area).
  Semi-Restricted Zone: Area requiring scrubs and cap (OT reception, ICU anteroom).

4. HAND HYGIENE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 POLICY STATEMENT

Hand hygiene is the single most important measure to prevent HAIs. Compliance
with hand hygiene is mandatory for ALL staff entering OT or ICU areas.
Zero-tolerance for non-compliance applies in these zones.

4.2 THE FIVE MOMENTS OF HAND HYGIENE (WHO Framework)

  Moment 1 — Before touching a patient
  Moment 2 — Before a clean or aseptic procedure
  Moment 3 — After body fluid exposure risk
  Moment 4 — After touching a patient
  Moment 5 — After touching patient surroundings

4.3 TECHNIQUE

  Alcohol-Based Hand Rub (ABHR) — 70% isopropyl alcohol formulation:
    - Apply 3 mL (approximately 2 pumps) to dry hands
    - Rub for 20-30 seconds using the WHO 6-step technique
    - Allow to air dry — do NOT wipe
    - Preferred method for routine hand hygiene in OT and ICU

  Soap and Water (Surgical Scrub):
    - Mandatory before first surgical case of the day
    - Required after visible soiling or contact with Clostridioides difficile cases
    - Nail pick used before the first scrub of the day
    - Scrub duration: 3-5 minutes (timed scrub using scrub sink timer)
    - Arms scrubbed to 2 inches above the elbow
    - Hands held higher than elbows after scrub; never touch non-sterile surfaces

4.4 HAND HYGIENE AUDITS

  - Weekly audits conducted by the Infection Control Nurse (ICN) using the
    WHO Hand Hygiene Observation Tool.
  - Target compliance: >= 90% for OT and ICU staff.
  - Results reported to Unit Head monthly and to ICC quarterly.
  - Repeat non-compliance (< 70% individual score for 2 consecutive months)
    escalated to Department Head and HR for documented counselling.

4.5 HAND HYGIENE INFRASTRUCTURE

  - ABHR dispensers placed at every OT bay entry, scrub sink station, and
    at every ICU bed space (head and foot).
  - ABHR refill monitored by nursing in-charge; shortage reported to Materials
    Management (ext. 3400) within 2 hours.
  - Soap dispensers and single-use paper towels maintained at all ICU sinks.
  - Artificial nails, nail polish, and hand jewelry (rings, bracelets) are
    prohibited in OT and ICU areas.

5. OPERATION THEATRE (OT) INFECTION CONTROL PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 OT ZONE CLASSIFICATION AND ACCESS CONTROL

  Zone              | Access Requirement                  | Who May Enter
  ------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------------------------
  Outer Zone        | Street clothes acceptable           | All hospital staff, patients
  Semi-Restricted   | OT scrubs, cap, surgical mask       | OT-scheduled staff only
  Restricted        | Scrubs, cap, mask, shoe covers      | Credentialed OT staff
  Sterile Zone      | Full sterile attire + sterile gloves| Scrubbed surgical team only

  - OT entry/exit is controlled via biometric access (OT Reception, ext. 2201).
  - Visitors and observers require written approval from the OT In-charge and
    Chief of Surgery. Maximum 1 observer per case; observers remain in the
    restricted zone only.

5.2 PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT (PPE) IN OT

  All OT personnel must wear:
    [ ] OT-specific scrubs (not worn outside the OT complex)
    [ ] Surgical cap — covering all hair completely
    [ ] Surgical mask (ASTM Level 3 or equivalent) — replaced between cases
        or when visibly soiled/wet
    [ ] Shoe covers — replaced on leaving and re-entering the OT complex
    [ ] Sterile gloves (scrubbed team members)
    [ ] Eye protection / face shield — mandatory for procedures with splash risk

  Double-gloving is recommended for:
    - Procedures > 2 hours duration
    - Orthopedic implant surgery
    - Cases with known or suspected bloodborne pathogen risk (HIV, HBV, HCV)

  PPE Removal Order (Doffing):
    Gloves → Eye protection → Gown → Mask/Cap → Hand hygiene

5.3 OT ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROLS

  Temperature and Humidity:
    - OT temperature: 18°C – 24°C (±2°C tolerance)
    - Relative humidity: 40% – 60%
    - Monitored continuously via HVAC system; daily log maintained by
      Biomedical Engineering (ext. 3600)
    - Deviations > tolerance threshold must be reported and corrective action
      taken before the next elective case

  Air Quality and Ventilation:
    - OT suites are maintained at positive pressure relative to adjacent corridors
    - Minimum 20 air changes per hour (ACH); laminar flow in designated OTs
      (OT 4, 5, 6 — Orthopedic and Cardiac suites)
    - HEPA filters (99.97% efficiency at 0.3 microns) inspected quarterly;
      replaced annually or as per manufacturer recommendation
    - Air ducts cleaned semi-annually; documented in Biomedical Engineering log

  Surfaces and Equipment:
    - All horizontal surfaces cleaned and disinfected between each surgical case
    - Terminal cleaning performed at end of each OT day list
    - Approved disinfectants: 1% sodium hypochlorite for general surfaces;
      2% glutaraldehyde for heat-sensitive instruments (only where autoclave
      is contraindicated)
    - Floors: mopped with 0.5% chlorine solution after each case; terminal
      mopping with 1% chlorine at end of day

5.4 SURGICAL SITE INFECTION (SSI) PREVENTION BUNDLE

  The following bundle elements are mandatory for all surgical cases and
  must be documented in the OT Checklist (HealthConnect — OT Module):

  Pre-operative:
    [ ] Surgical site hair removal by clipper (NOT razor) — within 2 hours of surgery
    [ ] Skin preparation with 2% Chlorhexidine Gluconate (CHG) + 70% alcohol
        (Chloraprep or equivalent); allowed to dry completely before draping
    [ ] Surgical antibiotic prophylaxis (SAP) administered within 60 minutes
        of incision (within 120 minutes for vancomycin/fluoroquinolones)
    [ ] SAP agent and dose confirmed per Apollo Antimicrobial Stewardship
        Guidelines (AH-CHN-AMS-001); default: Injection Cefazolin 2g IV
    [ ] Patient normothermia maintained (core temp >= 36°C); warming blanket
        used as indicated
    [ ] Glycaemic control: Blood glucose < 180 mg/dL pre-operatively for all
        patients; mandatory check for diabetics and cardiac surgery patients

  Intra-operative:
    [ ] Sterile draping verified before incision
    [ ] Glove change after every 90 minutes or after any known contamination
    [ ] Instrument counts completed and documented (opening, closing, and
        final counts by scrub nurse)
    [ ] Minimise OT traffic during the case — doors kept closed

  Post-operative:
    [ ] Wound covered with sterile dressing in OT; not disturbed for 48 hours
        unless clinical indication
    [ ] SAP discontinued within 24 hours of surgery (48 hours for cardiac surgery)
    [ ] SSI surveillance initiated — 30-day follow-up via OPD or phone for
        all elective cases; 90-day follow-up for implant surgeries

5.5 STERILIZATION AND INSTRUMENT MANAGEMENT

  Sterilization Methods (in order of preference):
    1. Steam Autoclave (High-pressure saturated steam, 134°C, 3 min / 121°C, 15 min)
       — Standard for all heat/moisture-tolerant instruments
    2. Ethylene Oxide (ETO) sterilization — for heat/moisture-sensitive devices
       — Central Sterile Supply Department (CSSD), Basement Level
    3. Hydrogen Peroxide Plasma (STERRAD) — for endoscopes and minimally
       invasive surgical instruments
    4. 2% Glutaraldehyde (Cidex) — High-level disinfection only; NOT sterilization
       — Restricted to scopes not amenable to STERRAD

  Packaging and Labelling:
    - All sterile packs labelled with: instrument name, sterilization date,
      expiry date, autoclave cycle number, and technician ID
    - Packs with compromised integrity (tears, wetness) must be rejected and
      re-sterilized regardless of labelling date

  Biological Indicators:
    - Geobacillus stearothermophilus spore strips tested weekly for autoclaves
    - STERRAD biological indicator run with every load
    - Positive biological indicator: halt sterilizer use; notify CSSD Supervisor
      and Biomedical Engineering; quarantine all loads since last negative BI

5.6 OT SCHEDULING AND INFECTIOUS CASES

  - Cases involving active infections (e.g., wet gangrene, perforated viscus,
    Necrotising Fasciitis) must be scheduled as the LAST case of the day
    in a designated OT bay.
  - Known MRSA, VRE, or CRE-positive patients: notify OT In-charge at least
    12 hours in advance; contact precautions implemented in the OT.
  - Dedicated instruments set aside for infectious cases; bagged separately
    for CSSD with biohazard label.
  - Enhanced terminal cleaning with 1% hypochlorite after infectious cases,
    followed by fumigation with formaldehyde (as per CSSD protocol AH-CHN-CSSD-002)
    if warranted.

6. ICU INFECTION CONTROL PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 ICU ZONE AND ACCESS CONTROL

  - ICU operates on a closed-unit model.
  - Access restricted to: ICU medical team, designated nursing staff, assigned
    paramedical staff, and approved family visitors.
  - Visitor policy: maximum 2 visitors per patient, during visiting hours
    (10:00 AM – 11:00 AM and 5:00 PM – 6:00 PM); wearing of surgical mask
    and hand hygiene at entry is mandatory for all visitors.
  - All staff must change into ICU-specific scrubs at the anteroom; street
    clothes are not permitted inside the ICU.

6.2 STANDARD AND TRANSMISSION-BASED PRECAUTIONS

  Standard Precautions (ALL patients, ALL times):
    - Hand hygiene at all 5 WHO moments
    - Gloves for contact with blood, body fluids, mucous membranes, non-intact skin
    - Gown/apron for procedures with splash/spray risk
    - Surgical mask + eye protection for splash/aerosol-generating procedures
    - Safe sharps handling — no recapping of needles; sharps bins at bedside

  Contact Precautions (MRSA, VRE, CRE, C. difficile, scabies):
    - Single room or cohorting with same organism
    - Dedicated patient equipment (stethoscope, BP cuff, thermometer)
    - Gloves + gown for ALL room entry
    - Soap and water hand hygiene (not ABHR) for C. difficile
    - Contact precaution signage displayed at room entry

  Droplet Precautions (Influenza, COVID-19, RSV, pertussis):
    - Surgical mask for staff within 1 metre of patient
    - Patient placed in single room; door may remain open
    - Patient masked during transport

  Airborne Precautions (Active/Suspected TB, measles, varicella, COVID-19 AGP):
    - N95 respirator (fit-tested) for all staff entering room
    - Negative pressure room (ICU Room 12 and 13 designated; 12 ACH minimum)
    - Door must remain closed at all times
    - Patient transport minimised; N95 on patient during transport

6.3 CENTRAL LINE-ASSOCIATED BLOODSTREAM INFECTION (CLABSI) PREVENTION BUNDLE

  Insertion Bundle:
    [ ] Hand hygiene before insertion
    [ ] Maximum sterile barrier precautions (sterile gown, gloves, cap, mask,
        large sterile drape) for all CVC insertions — no exceptions
    [ ] Skin antisepsis with CHG-alcohol (2% CHG + 70% isopropyl) — dry time
        minimum 30 seconds
    [ ] Preferred insertion site: subclavian vein (lowest infection risk)
        Femoral vein insertion avoided unless no alternative
    [ ] Use of ultrasound guidance documented in procedure note
    [ ] CVC insertion checklist signed by inserting physician and bedside nurse

  Maintenance Bundle (Daily):
    [ ] Need for CVC assessed and documented daily — remove if no longer needed
    [ ] Dressing intact and dry — change every 7 days (chlorhexidine-impregnated
        dressing) or immediately if soiled/loose
    [ ] Needleless connectors (hubs) disinfected with CHG-alcohol for
        minimum 15 seconds before each access ("scrub the hub")
    [ ] Blood draws through CVC minimised; peripheral stick preferred
    [ ] Line not used for TPN unless dedicated port; TPN line changed every 24 hours

6.4 VENTILATOR-ASSOCIATED PNEUMONIA (VAP) PREVENTION BUNDLE

  [ ] Head of bed elevation: 30°–45° at all times (except contraindicated)
      — Angle measured and documented every nursing shift
  [ ] Oral care with 0.12% Chlorhexidine gluconate solution every 6 hours
  [ ] Sedation vacation daily — assess readiness for extubation every morning
  [ ] Daily spontaneous breathing trial (SBT) — documented by intensivist
  [ ] Subglottic secretion drainage (for patients ventilated > 72 hours;
      SSD-equipped ETTs used by default for planned ventilation)
  [ ] Heat and moisture exchangers (HME) changed every 5-7 days or when visibly soiled
  [ ] Ventilator circuits: not routinely changed — changed only when visibly
      soiled or malfunctioning (per CDC/HICPAC recommendation)
  [ ] Stress ulcer prophylaxis and DVT prophylaxis per ICU protocol

6.5 CATHETER-ASSOCIATED URINARY TRACT INFECTION (CAUTI) PREVENTION BUNDLE

  [ ] Urinary catheter inserted only when clinically indicated (criteria in
      HealthConnect — ICU Nursing Protocol ICU-NP-007)
  [ ] Smallest appropriate catheter size used (typically 14-16 Fr)
  [ ] Aseptic insertion technique; CHG-based cleaning of urethral meatus
  [ ] Closed drainage system maintained at all times — no disconnection of
      tubing without clinical necessity
  [ ] Drainage bag maintained below bladder level; never on the floor
  [ ] Catheter removed as soon as clinically appropriate — daily review mandatory
  [ ] Catheter days logged in HealthConnect for CAUTI rate calculation

6.6 ANTIMICROBIAL STEWARDSHIP IN ICU

  - All antimicrobial prescriptions in ICU require documentation of:
      * Indication
      * Anticipated duration
      * Culture/sensitivity status
  - De-escalation of antibiotics mandatory within 72 hours of culture results
    (unless clinical deterioration prevents it — must be documented)
  - Weekly antimicrobial review by ICU team + Infectious Disease consultant
    (Dr. Arjun Krishnaswamy, ext. 4218) — every Wednesday, 8:00 AM ward round
  - Broad-spectrum antibiotics (Meropenem, Colistin, Polymyxin B, Tigecycline)
    require co-signature from the Infectious Disease team or CMO before initiation
  - Culture-before-antibiotics policy: blood cultures (minimum 2 sets), urine,
    tracheal aspirate drawn BEFORE first antibiotic dose wherever possible

6.7 ICU ENVIRONMENTAL CLEANING PROTOCOL

  Frequency:
    - Bedside environment: cleaned and disinfected every 8 hours (each nursing shift)
    - Equipment surfaces (monitors, pumps, rails): wiped with 70% isopropyl
      alcohol wipes at shift change
    - Floor mopping: every 8 hours with 0.5% chlorine solution
    - Terminal cleaning (on patient discharge/transfer): 1% sodium hypochlorite
      on all surfaces; HEPA-filtered vacuuming of soft furnishings
    - UV-C disinfection: deployed for terminal cleaning of rooms vacated by
      patients with MDR organisms (MRSA, CRE, VRE)

  Equipment Decontamination:
    - Dedicated equipment per bed space where possible
    - Shared equipment (portable USG, echo machine, portable X-ray) disinfected
      with 70% alcohol wipes between each patient use
    - Biomedical engineering responsible for internal cleaning of shared equipment
      monthly — documented in equipment log

7. SURVEILLANCE AND REPORTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7.1 HAI SURVEILLANCE

  The Infection Control team conducts prospective surveillance for:
    - SSI rates (per 100 surgical procedures, by category)
    - CLABSI rates (per 1000 central line days)
    - CAUTI rates (per 1000 catheter days)
    - VAP rates (per 1000 ventilator days)

  Benchmark targets (aligned with NHSN percentiles):
    Indicator     | Apollo Target      | Action Threshold
    --------------|--------------------|-----------------------
    SSI Rate      | < 2 per 100 cases  | >= 3 per 100 cases
    CLABSI Rate   | < 1 per 1000 CL days | >= 2 per 1000 CL days
    CAUTI Rate    | < 2 per 1000 cath days | >= 3 per 1000 cath days
    VAP Rate      | < 5 per 1000 vent days | >= 8 per 1000 vent days

7.2 OUTBREAK INVESTIGATION

  A cluster of >= 2 related HAIs within 2 weeks is treated as a potential
  outbreak:
    - HICO notified immediately
    - Environmental swabs and cultures collected
    - Case-control investigation initiated
    - Cohorting of affected patients implemented
    - Enhanced cleaning protocol activated
    - ICC convened for emergency review within 48 hours
    - TNOCD (Tamil Nadu Occupational and Communicable Disease) notified if
      required under state regulations

7.3 MANDATORY REPORTING

  The following must be reported to the Hospital Infection Control Officer
  within 24 hours of identification:
    - Any confirmed CLABSI, VAP, or CAUTI in ICU
    - Any SSI requiring surgical re-intervention
    - Outbreak of any HAI (>=2 linked cases)
    - Any positive culture for CRE (Carbapenem-Resistant Enterobacteriaceae),
      CRKP, or CRAB (Carbapenem-Resistant Acinetobacter baumannii)
    - Needle-stick or sharps injuries (also report via HR — ext. 3900 — within
      4 hours for post-exposure prophylaxis if indicated)

8. STAFF HEALTH AND VACCINATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  All OT and ICU staff must maintain the following vaccinations (verified at
  onboarding and reviewed annually by Occupational Health, ext. 4300):

    Vaccine                  | Requirement
    -------------------------|---------------------------------------------------
    Hepatitis B              | Complete 3-dose series; anti-HBs titer >= 10 IU/L
    Influenza                | Annual dose (October – November each year)
    COVID-19                 | Primary series + boosters per current MOHFW advisory
    Varicella                | Documented immunity or 2-dose series
    MMR                      | Documented immunity or 2-dose series
    Tdap                     | One dose (if not received in previous 10 years)

  Fitness to Work:
    - Staff with active skin infections, open wounds, or acute respiratory
      illness must report to Occupational Health before commencing duty in OT/ICU.
    - Staff with confirmed MRSA nasal carriage will be decolonized per
      occupational health protocol before returning to sterile areas.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. TRAINING AND COMPETENCY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - All new OT and ICU staff must complete the Apollo IPC Induction Training
    within 30 days of joining (in-person, conducted by HICO team; 4 hours).
  - Annual refresher training mandatory for all staff; completion tracked in
    the Apollo Learning Management System (LMS).
  - Competency assessments:
      * Hand hygiene technique — observed annually
      * PPE donning and doffing — observed at induction and annually
      * Aseptic non-touch technique (ANTT) — annual skills lab assessment
  - Non-completion of mandatory training reported to Unit Head.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. WASTE MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Biomedical waste in OT and ICU must be segregated at source per
  Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016 (amended 2019):

    Colour Code | Container Type      | Waste Category
    ------------|---------------------|------------------------------------------
    Yellow      | Bag (non-chlorinated)| Human anatomical waste, soiled dressings,
                |                     | blood bags, body fluids
    Red         | Bag (non-chlorinated)| Contaminated recyclables — IV sets,
                |                     | catheters, gloves
    White       | Puncture-proof box  | Sharps (needles, blades, broken glass)
    Blue        | Box                 | Glassware, metallic implants/bodies

  - Sharps bins must never exceed 3/4 capacity before sealing and disposal.
  - Biomedical waste collected from OT and ICU twice daily (10:00 AM and 8:00 PM)
    by designated housekeeping staff in PPE.
  - Liquid waste (blood, fluids) disposed via designated drain points connected
    to effluent treatment system — never via general sink.
